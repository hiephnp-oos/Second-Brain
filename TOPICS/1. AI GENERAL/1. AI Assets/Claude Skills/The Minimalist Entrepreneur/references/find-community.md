# Find Community

## Purpose

Identify and evaluate communities as the foundation for building a minimalist business.

---

## When to Apply

- No clear business idea
- Exploring opportunities
- Unsure where to start
- Considering multiple directions without focus

---

## Core Principle

Start with **community, not product**.

A strong business emerges from:
- deep familiarity with a group of people
- repeated exposure to their problems
- long-term willingness to serve them

You do not need to find a community — you are already part of several.

---

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Identify Existing Communities

List communities you already belong to:

- Professional (industry, coworkers, networks)
- Hobby / interest groups
- Online platforms (Reddit, Discord, forums, etc.)
- Local / offline groups
- Identity-based groups (alumni, region, lifestyle)

---

### Step 2 — Map Attention Channels

Identify where these communities are active:

- Platforms (Reddit, Facebook groups, Slack, Discord, X, YouTube, etc.)
- Communication patterns (posts, comments, chats, events)

---

### Step 3 — Extract Problems

For each community, identify:

- Repeated complaints
- Friction in workflows
- Workarounds people use
- Questions asked frequently

Focus on **persistent, recurring pain points**

---

### Step 4 — Evaluate Each Community

Apply filters:

- **Authenticity**  
  Are you a real member (not outsider)?

- **Pain level**  
  Is the problem strong enough that people would pay?

- **Access**  
  Can you directly reach these people?

- **Scope**  
  Niche enough to dominate, but large enough to sustain

---

### Step 5 — Select Target Community

Narrow down to **1–3 communities** based on:

- strongest problem intensity
- highest personal alignment
- easiest access

---

## Anti-patterns

- Starting from an abstract “idea” instead of real people
- Targeting overly broad groups (“everyone”, “users”, “SMEs”)
- Choosing based on market size instead of familiarity
- Attempting to create a new community from scratch
- Skipping observation and jumping to selling

---

## Output

Produce a shortlist (1–3 communities), each with:

- Community description
- Specific recurring problem
- Your connection to the community
- Where the community gathers (online/offline)
