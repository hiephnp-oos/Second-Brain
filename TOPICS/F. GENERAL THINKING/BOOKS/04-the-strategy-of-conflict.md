# 04 — The Strategy of Conflict

**Source:** Thomas C. Schelling, *The Strategy of Conflict*  
**Review status:** Core strategic-conflict source

## Core thesis
Conflict is not only about opposing interests. It also contains opportunities for coordination, bargaining, deterrence, signaling, and manipulation of expectations.

## Reusable knowledge
- Outcomes depend on expectations about what each side will do.
- Coordination can emerge even when interests are partly opposed.
- Credible commitment is often more important than stated intention.
- A threat works when the threatened action is believable and connected to incentives.
- A promise can alter behavior when the promised response is credible.
- Strategic advantage can come from deliberately limiting one's future options.
- Focal points help actors coordinate when communication is incomplete.
- Brinkmanship relies on controlled risk and uncertainty; it is powerful but dangerous.
- Signals are useful when costly or otherwise difficult to fake.
- Bargaining power is affected by alternatives, expectations, and the ability to commit.

## Application
For conflict or negotiation, map:
`interests → alternatives → commitments → signals → focal points → credible responses`

## Limitation
Schelling's examples often involve high-stakes conflict. Transfer the mechanism, not the surface analogy. Do not treat escalation or brinkmanship as universally desirable.
