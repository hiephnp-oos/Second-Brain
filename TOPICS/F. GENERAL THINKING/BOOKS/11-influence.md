# 11 — Influence

**Source:** Robert B. Cialdini, *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion*  
**Review status:** Core influence source

## Core thesis
People often use automatic social heuristics to decide what to do. Persuasion becomes more effective when these mechanisms are activated, and defensive reasoning improves when they are recognized.

## Reusable knowledge
- Reciprocity: people feel pressure to return benefits, favors, or concessions.
- Commitment and consistency: prior commitments can shape later behavior.
- Social proof: people use others' behavior as evidence, especially under uncertainty.
- Liking: similarity, familiarity, cooperation, and positive association can increase compliance.
- Authority: credible expertise or status can influence decisions.
- Scarcity: perceived loss of access can increase value.
- Automatic response patterns are efficient but can be exploited.
- Ethical use requires making the underlying choice genuinely beneficial and avoiding deceptive manipulation.

## Defensive checklist
When someone is persuading you, ask:
1. Which heuristic is being activated?
2. Would I make the same decision without the cue?
3. Is the authority actually relevant?
4. Is scarcity real or manufactured?
5. Am I complying because of a prior commitment that should be reconsidered?

## Limitation
The principles are mechanisms, not deterministic laws. Context, culture, incentives, and individual differences matter.
