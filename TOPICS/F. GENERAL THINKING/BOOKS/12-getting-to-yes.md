# 12 — Getting to Yes

**Source:** Roger Fisher & William Ury, *Getting to Yes*  
**Review status:** Core negotiation source

## Core thesis
Effective negotiation should focus on interests and objective standards rather than positional combat. A negotiator should also protect themselves with a strong BATNA.

## Reusable knowledge
- Separate people from the problem.
- Focus on interests, not positions.
- Invent options for mutual gain before deciding.
- Insist on objective criteria where possible.
- BATNA defines the value of walking away and prevents accepting a worse deal merely to reach agreement.
- A strong BATNA increases bargaining power without requiring aggression.
- Negotiation is not only about dividing value; creating value can expand the set of possible agreements.
- When the other side is difficult, change the interaction from positional attack/defense toward problem-solving and standards.

## Practical sequence
`Interests → BATNAs → options → objective criteria → agreement / walk-away`

## Limitation
Principled negotiation works best when the parties can communicate and some shared standards exist. Power asymmetry, deception, time pressure, or non-negotiable constraints can require additional tactics.
