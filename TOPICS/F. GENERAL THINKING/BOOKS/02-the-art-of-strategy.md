# 02 — The Art of Strategy

**Source:** Avinash K. Dixit & Barry J. Nalebuff, *The Art of Strategy*  
**Review status:** Core operational source

## Core thesis
Strategic thinking becomes useful when game-theoretic ideas are translated into practical reasoning about incentives, expectations, timing, information, and commitment.

## Reusable knowledge
- Model the game before choosing a move.
- Separate simultaneous from sequential interaction.
- In sequential games, reason backward from the final decision.
- In simultaneous games, map best responses and likely equilibria.
- Change incentives or information when direct persuasion is weak.
- Commitment can be more powerful than verbal promises.
- A strategic move can create value by narrowing your own future choices if that restriction is credible.
- Information can be valuable because it changes the other side's behavior, not merely because it improves your private estimate.
- In repeated games, future consequences support cooperation and reputation.
- When uncertainty is central, ask what information each side has and how actions signal hidden information.

## Practical sequence
`Players → choices → information → incentives → responses → equilibrium / path → strategic move`

## Limitation
Game models are abstractions. The objective is not to force every situation into a formal game, but to expose interaction effects that ordinary single-agent reasoning misses.
