# 14 — Thinking in Bets

**Source:** Annie Duke, *Thinking in Bets*  
**Review status:** Core decision-quality source

## Core thesis
Good decisions should be evaluated by the quality of the reasoning available at the time, not by whether the eventual outcome happened to be good. Treat uncertain decisions as bets and make beliefs probabilistic.

## Reusable knowledge
- Separate decision quality from outcome quality (“resulting”).
- State uncertainty explicitly rather than hiding behind binary certainty.
- Treat beliefs as hypotheses that can be updated.
- Seek disagreement because dissent can expose hidden assumptions.
- Use a trusted decision group or “buddy system” to improve calibration and reduce self-serving reasoning.
- Backcasting helps identify paths to a desired future.
- Premortems help expose plausible failure paths before commitment.
- Precommitment can protect future decisions from predictable emotional or cognitive drift.
- Record important decisions and later compare reasoning with outcomes without rewriting history.

## Application
For a consequential decision record:
`choice → alternatives → probabilities → assumptions → downside → disconfirming evidence → precommitments`

## Limitation
Probability estimates are not automatically accurate because they are expressed numerically. Calibration requires feedback and scoring.
