# 05 — The Evolution of Cooperation

**Source:** Robert Axelrod, *The Evolution of Cooperation*  
**Review status:** Core cooperation source

## Core thesis
Cooperation can emerge among self-interested actors when interaction is repeated and future consequences are sufficiently important.

## Reusable knowledge
- One-shot incentives can favor defection even when mutual cooperation is better.
- Repetition changes the payoff structure by making future consequences relevant.
- Reciprocity can sustain cooperation without requiring central control.
- A cooperative strategy must balance willingness to cooperate with resistance to exploitation.
- Tit-for-tat is historically important because it is simple, initially cooperative, responsive, forgiving under some variants, and clear; it is not a universal optimum.
- Reputation and retaliation affect future behavior.
- Noise changes the strategy problem: rigid retaliation can create downward spirals.
- Cooperation depends on the shadow of the future, the probability of interaction continuing, and the cost of retaliation.

## Application
For recurring relationships ask:
1. Is the interaction repeated?
2. Can behavior be observed?
3. Can responses be linked to prior behavior?
4. Is future interaction valuable enough?
5. What retaliation or forgiveness rule is credible?

## Limitation
Tournament results are conditional on the payoff structure, noise, strategy population, and environment. Do not reduce cooperation to “always use tit-for-tat.”
