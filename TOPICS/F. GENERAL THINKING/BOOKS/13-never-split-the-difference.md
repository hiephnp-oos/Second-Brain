# 13 — Never Split the Difference

**Source:** Chris Voss, *Never Split the Difference*  
**Review status:** Tactical negotiation source

## Core thesis
In difficult negotiations, emotional dynamics, perceived control, and hidden constraints often matter more than abstract rational bargaining. Tactical empathy and calibrated questions can uncover information while preserving leverage.

## Reusable knowledge
- Slow the interaction down when emotions or pressure are distorting judgment.
- Mirroring encourages the other side to elaborate.
- Labels make emotions or concerns explicit without necessarily agreeing with them.
- Tactical empathy means demonstrating understanding of the other side's perspective and emotional state.
- An accusation audit surfaces negative perceptions before they become hidden resistance.
- “No” can create psychological safety and reveal boundaries; do not treat every “yes” as commitment.
- Calibrated open-ended questions can transfer problem-solving pressure to the counterpart.
- Execution needs explicit confirmation, not just apparent agreement.
- Hidden information or “black swans” can radically change the negotiation; search for unknown unknowns.

## Limitation
The book is a practitioner account, not a controlled theory of all negotiations. Tactics should be adapted to culture, relationship, power, and ethical constraints.
