# 06 — Thinking in Systems

**Source:** Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems*  
**Review status:** Core operational source

## Core thesis
Behavior is often generated by system structure rather than isolated events or individual intent. To change persistent outcomes, inspect stocks, flows, feedback, delays, information, rules, and goals.

## Reusable knowledge
- Stocks accumulate; flows change stocks.
- Reinforcing loops amplify change.
- Balancing loops resist change and seek stability.
- Delays can create oscillation, overshoot, or unintended consequences.
- Nonlinear relationships make local intuition unreliable.
- Information flows are structural: who knows what, when, and with what feedback matters.
- Rules and incentives shape behavior more deeply than slogans.
- System boundaries are choices; excluding important feedback can produce misleading explanations.
- Leverage is often higher in goals, rules, information flows, and paradigms than in numerical parameters.
- Common traps include policy resistance, shifting the burden, fixes that fail, and escalation.

## Application
When a problem repeats, draw:
`stock → flows → feedback loops → delays → rules/goals`

Then ask where a small structural intervention could change the behavior of the whole system.

## Limitation
The leverage hierarchy is a heuristic, not a guarantee that a high-level intervention will be easy, safe, or effective.
