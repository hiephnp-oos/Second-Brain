# 15 — Good Strategy, Bad Strategy

**Source:** Richard P. Rumelt, *Good Strategy/Bad Strategy*  
**Review status:** Core strategy source

## Core thesis
Strategy is not ambition, vision statements, or a list of goals. Good strategy identifies the critical obstacle, chooses an approach for dealing with it, and coordinates coherent actions around that choice.

## Strategy kernel
1. **Diagnosis** — define the essential challenge and simplify reality around what matters.
2. **Guiding policy** — establish the overall approach to overcoming the diagnosed challenge.
3. **Coherent action** — coordinate mutually reinforcing actions that implement the policy.

## Reusable knowledge
- A goal describes a desired state; strategy explains how to overcome the obstacle between here and there.
- Concentration and focus can create leverage.
- Proximate objectives are concrete enough to guide action when the ultimate objective is too ambiguous.
- Advantage comes from asymmetries and differences that can be exploited, not from generic superiority.
- Design matters: good strategy coordinates choices so that the whole is stronger than isolated actions.
- Growth is not itself a strategy.
- Inertia and organizational habits can preserve obsolete positions.
- Strategy must make choices; a plan that tries to do everything usually lacks strategic focus.

## Practical test
Ask:
1. What is the critical obstacle?
2. What is our diagnosis?
3. What policy changes how we will address it?
4. Which actions reinforce each other?
5. What are we deliberately not doing?

## Limitation
The kernel is a powerful reasoning structure, not a substitute for domain knowledge, execution capability, or evidence.
