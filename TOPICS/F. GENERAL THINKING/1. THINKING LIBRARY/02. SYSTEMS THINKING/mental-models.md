# Systems Thinking — Mental Models

## SYS-MM-01 — Structure before event
When a problem repeats, search for the structure generating the pattern.

## SYS-MM-02 — Map stock and flow
Ask what accumulates, what changes it, and what history is embedded in the current state.

## SYS-MM-03 — Trace feedback loops
Identify reinforcing and balancing loops before predicting an intervention.

## SYS-MM-04 — Put delays on the map
A correct intervention can look ineffective when effects arrive later than the decision cycle.

## SYS-MM-05 — Test for nonlinearity
Do not assume twice the input produces twice the output.

## SYS-MM-06 — Follow information
Find who receives the signal, what they do with it, and what is missing or delayed.

## SYS-MM-07 — Prefer structural leverage
Changing information, feedback, rules or goals can outperform repeated symptom treatment.

## SYS-MM-08 — Explain policy resistance
If an intervention triggers counteracting responses, model the loop instead of merely increasing intervention strength.

## SYS-MM-09 — Preserve resilience
Do not optimize a system so tightly that small disturbances become catastrophic.

## SYS-MM-10 — Recheck boundaries
A model can fail because relevant actors, resources, time horizons or feedback loops were excluded.

## SYS-MM-11 — Behavior is a pattern over time
A snapshot can hide accumulation, oscillation, delay or adaptation.

## SYS-MM-12 — Local optimization can damage the whole
Improving one component may worsen system-level performance when components are coupled.

## SYS-MM-13 — Separate stock state from flow rate
A high or low stock reflects accumulated history; changing today's flow does not instantly reset the stock. Track both levels and rates.

## SYS-MM-14 — Compare competing balancing loops
When several loops regulate one stock toward different goals, identify each goal, response strength and delay before predicting the equilibrium or trajectory.

## SYS-MM-15 — Model coupled stocks together
When stocks affect one another through flows or feedback, draw the coupled structure before optimizing either stock independently.

## SYS-MM-16 — Look for shifting loop dominance
A reinforcing loop may dominate early growth while balancing feedback or limits dominate later. Explain the trajectory across phases rather than extrapolating one phase indefinitely.

## SYS-MM-17 — Test for overshoot before intervening harder
If feedback is delayed, stronger correction can arrive after the system has already crossed its target. Check response time and accumulated momentum before increasing control.

## SYS-MM-18 — Optimize for resilience, not just stability
Ask whether the system can absorb and recover from disturbance, not only whether its current output looks constant.

## SYS-MM-19 — Protect self-organization
Before adding controls, check whether simple rules, local adaptation, diversity and experimentation could solve the problem more effectively than centralized micromanagement.

## SYS-MM-20 — Check whether the hierarchy serves the whole
Map subsystem goals against the larger system's purpose. Look for local optimization, information bottlenecks or a higher level that undermines the units it depends on.

## SYS-MM-21 — Move from event to behavior to structure
Describe the recurring pattern over time, then identify stocks, flows and feedback that could generate it before selecting a fix.

## SYS-MM-22 — Audit the boundary and externalized costs
List excluded actors, sources, sinks, time horizons and side effects. Check whether the apparent solution only moves the problem outside the model.

## SYS-MM-23 — Look for interacting limits
Identify several constraints and test how growth changes them or shifts which one dominates. Do not assume a single fixed limit explains every phase.

## SYS-MM-24 — Model bounded rationality
For each actor, specify available information, attention, time, local goal and perceived alternatives. Use these limits to explain aggregate behavior.

## SYS-MM-25 — Diagnose the trap before choosing the intervention
Classify the recurring feedback pattern first. Similar symptoms can arise from different structures and require different ways out.

## SYS-MM-26 — Resolve policy resistance through goal alignment
When actors counteract one another, surface their goals and search for a mutually acceptable higher-level objective before increasing force.

## SYS-MM-27 — Restore feedback in shared-resource systems
If users benefit from use while others share the cost, make resource condition and use consequences visible and establish workable collective rules.

## SYS-MM-28 — Interrupt escalation
When each side's response becomes the other's trigger, stop matching the increase or introduce negotiated balancing constraints.

## SYS-MM-29 — Prevent success from compounding unfairly
Check whether today's winners receive advantages that guarantee future wins. Consider diversification, limits or rewards that do not pre-load the next competition.

## SYS-MM-30 — Rebuild capability instead of extending relief
If an intervention reduces symptoms while weakening the system's ability to solve the underlying problem, restore that capacity and plan how dependence will end.

## SYS-MM-31 — Treat rule beating as design feedback
When people game a rule, investigate the incentives and purpose before adding enforcement. Revise rules so successful adaptation advances their real purpose.

## SYS-MM-32 — Test goals against real outcomes
Ask whether the system's indicators measure the intended welfare or only a convenient proxy. Distinguish effort, compliance and output from the outcome that matters.

## SYS-MM-33 — Detect eroding standards
If targets are repeatedly reset to match declining performance, compare results with a stable reference and use credible high performance to raise the standard.


## SYS-MM-34 — Treat leverage rankings as hypotheses
Use the leverage-point hierarchy to generate candidate interventions, then test it against the system's actual structure, constraints, delays and resistance.

## SYS-MM-35 — Change parameters only when they matter
Before adjusting a number, check whether it is near a critical threshold and whether the deeper feedback, rules or goals remain unchanged.

## SYS-MM-36 — Size buffers relative to flows
Evaluate whether a buffer can absorb the scale and duration of disturbances, and whether maintaining it creates costs or hides a structural problem.

## SYS-MM-37 — Distinguish physical structure from feedback structure
When an intervention requires rebuilding stocks and flows, compare its cost and delay with changes to information, rules or feedback that may alter behavior sooner.

## SYS-MM-38 — Match delay to system dynamics
Map the delay from action to observation and response. A delay is important relative to how quickly the system itself changes.

## SYS-MM-39 — Check balancing-loop strength
Ask whether corrective feedback is timely and strong enough relative to disturbances, and whether the signal is distorted or ignored.

## SYS-MM-40 — Check reinforcing-loop gain
Identify what compounds growth, decline or escalation, and test whether reducing the loop's gain changes the trajectory.

## SYS-MM-41 — Restore missing information flows
Map who has information, who needs it, when it arrives and what decision it can change.

## SYS-MM-42 — Inspect rules before blaming actors
When behavior is systematic, inspect incentives, constraints and permissions that make it rational or rewarding within the system.

## SYS-MM-43 — Preserve self-organization
Allow diversity, experimentation and local adaptation when they improve the system's capacity to learn and respond.

## SYS-MM-44 — Test the operative goal
Compare the stated purpose, rewarded indicators and actual system behavior. A goal change may have more effect than optimizing existing processes.

## SYS-MM-45 — Surface the paradigm beneath the design
Identify assumptions that define what counts as a problem, a solution or a legitimate goal.

## SYS-MM-46 — Hold paradigms lightly
Treat models and worldviews as useful but incomplete. Be willing to revise assumptions rather than defend them as absolute truth.

## SYS-MM-47 — Observe the system before intervening
Establish the historical pattern and time scale before making a change; distinguish real trends from impressions.

## SYS-MM-48 — Externalize and compare mental models
Write assumptions down, make causal diagrams and compare predictions with observed behavior.

## SYS-MM-49 — Make decision-relevant information accessible
Check whether people who influence outcomes receive accurate information early enough to act on it.

## SYS-MM-50 — Audit language and framing
Ask whether labels obscure stocks, flows, delays, relationships or the actual purpose of the system.

## SYS-MM-51 — Separate important outcomes from convenient metrics
Use quantitative measures as evidence, not as substitutes for qualities or outcomes that are harder to count.

## SYS-MM-52 — Adapt policy through feedback
Define expected effects, monitor real results and revise policy when feedback contradicts assumptions.

## SYS-MM-53 — Optimize for the whole
Evaluate local improvements for effects on the wider system, other actors and longer time horizons.

## SYS-MM-54 — Listen before forcing a design
Use the system's existing behavior and constraints as evidence. Prefer interventions that work with its dynamics where feasible.

## SYS-MM-55 — Align responsibility, information and consequences
Look for gaps between who makes a decision, who sees its effects and who bears the consequences.

## SYS-MM-56 — Practice epistemic humility
Assume models are incomplete, make uncertainty explicit and treat surprises as opportunities to learn.

## SYS-MM-57 — Simplify without deleting critical relationships
Choose the least complex model that still captures the causal interactions relevant to the question.

## SYS-MM-58 — Include delayed and long-term effects
Extend the time horizon until important accumulation, recovery and side effects become visible.

## SYS-MM-59 — Cross boundaries between disciplines
Bring in the knowledge needed to explain the whole system, even when it sits outside a single function or specialty.

## SYS-MM-60 — Expand the circle of consequences
Include indirect stakeholders, ecosystems and future generations when defining system boundaries and evaluating outcomes.

## SYS-MM-61 — Protect the underlying value
Check whether the system is producing a measurable proxy instead of the real welfare or purpose it was designed to support.
