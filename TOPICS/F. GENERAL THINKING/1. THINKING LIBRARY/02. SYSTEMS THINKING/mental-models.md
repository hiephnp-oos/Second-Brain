# Systems Thinking — Mental Models

## SYS-MM-01 — Structure before event
When a problem repeats, search for the structure generating the pattern.

## SYS-MM-02 — Map stock and flow
Ask what accumulates, what changes it, and what history is embedded in the current state.

## SYS-MM-03 — Trace feedback loops
Identify reinforcing and balancing loops before predicting an intervention.

## SYS-MM-04 — Put delays on the map
A correct intervention can look ineffective when effects arrive later than the decision cycle.

## SYS-MM-05 — Test for nonlinearity
Do not assume twice the input produces twice the output.

## SYS-MM-06 — Follow information
Find who receives the signal, what they do with it, and what is missing or delayed.

## SYS-MM-07 — Prefer structural leverage
Changing information, feedback, rules or goals can outperform repeated symptom treatment.

## SYS-MM-08 — Explain policy resistance
If an intervention triggers counteracting responses, model the loop instead of merely increasing intervention strength.

## SYS-MM-09 — Preserve resilience
Do not optimize a system so tightly that small disturbances become catastrophic.

## SYS-MM-10 — Recheck boundaries
A model can fail because relevant actors, resources, time horizons or feedback loops were excluded.

## SYS-MM-11 — Behavior is a pattern over time
A snapshot can hide accumulation, oscillation, delay or adaptation.

## SYS-MM-12 — Local optimization can damage the whole
Improving one component may worsen system-level performance when components are coupled.

## SYS-MM-13 — Separate stock state from flow rate
A high or low stock reflects accumulated history; changing today's flow does not instantly reset the stock. Track both levels and rates.

## SYS-MM-14 — Compare competing balancing loops
When several loops regulate one stock toward different goals, identify each goal, response strength and delay before predicting the equilibrium or trajectory.

## SYS-MM-15 — Model coupled stocks together
When stocks affect one another through flows or feedback, draw the coupled structure before optimizing either stock independently.

## SYS-MM-16 — Look for shifting loop dominance
A reinforcing loop may dominate early growth while balancing feedback or limits dominate later. Explain the trajectory across phases rather than extrapolating one phase indefinitely.

## SYS-MM-17 — Test for overshoot before intervening harder
If feedback is delayed, stronger correction can arrive after the system has already crossed its target. Check response time and accumulated momentum before increasing control.
