# Systems Thinking — Concepts

Source: Book 06 *Thinking in Systems* — Donella H. Meadows.

## Core structure
A system is a set of elements, interconnections and a purpose whose structure generates characteristic behavior. Events are outputs of deeper structure, not always isolated causes.

## Stocks and flows
Stocks accumulate or deplete over time. Flows change stocks. The state of a stock depends on the history of inflows and outflows, creating inertia and delays between intervention and visible effect.

## Feedback
Reinforcing feedback amplifies change. Balancing feedback counteracts change toward a goal or constraint. Multiple loops can interact and produce growth, stabilization, oscillation or collapse.

## Delays and nonlinearity
Delays separate action from consequence. Nonlinear relationships make proportional intuition unreliable.

## Information, rules and goals
Information flows, rules, constraints and goals are structural variables. Changing them can alter behavior without changing physical elements.

## Resilience and leverage
Systems can adapt and reorganize. Leverage can lie in information flows, feedback structures, rules, goals and deeper assumptions. The hierarchy is a heuristic, not a guarantee.

## Practical synthesis
Observe behavior over time → identify stocks/flows → map feedback → locate delays/nonlinearity → inspect information/rules/goals → find structural leverage → test side effects and policy resistance.
