# Systems Thinking — Concepts

Source: Book 06 *Thinking in Systems* — Donella H. Meadows.

## Core structure
A system is a set of elements, interconnections and a purpose whose structure generates characteristic behavior. Events are outputs of deeper structure, not always isolated causes. The same elements can behave differently when their relationships, rules or purpose change.

## Stocks and flows
Stocks accumulate or deplete over time. Flows change stocks. The state of a stock depends on the history of inflows and outflows, creating inertia and delays between intervention and visible effect. A flow can change quickly while the stock it affects changes gradually.

## Feedback
A feedback loop closes a causal chain: the state of a stock affects decisions or physical processes that then change the stock. Reinforcing feedback amplifies change; balancing feedback counteracts change relative to a goal or constraint. Multiple loops can interact, and their relative strengths can shift over time.

## Bathtub dynamics and dynamic equilibrium
A bathtub is a simple stock-and-flow model: water level is the stock, faucet inflow and drain outflow are flows. Equal inflow and outflow hold the stock steady even while material continues moving. A stock's level does not instantly match a change in flow; accumulated quantity and flow rates must be distinguished.

## One-stock system patterns
A single stock can be controlled by competing balancing loops with different goals, as in a thermostat balancing room temperature against both a desired setting and heat exchange with the environment. A single stock can also be driven by reinforcing growth while balancing constraints or depletion increasingly oppose it. The resulting behavior depends on loop structure, relative strength, limits and delays—not on the stock count alone.

## Two-stock system patterns
Two stocks can be coupled when a flow or feedback relationship links their rates of change. Population and productive capital, inventory and replenishment, or a resource and the capacity exploiting it can produce behavior that cannot be understood by analyzing either stock in isolation. Delays in information or response can cause overshoot and oscillation; reinforcing growth may dominate initially and then be constrained by balancing feedback or resource limits.

## Delays and nonlinearity
Delays separate action from consequence. Nonlinear relationships make proportional intuition unreliable. A delayed balancing loop may correct too late and create oscillation; a growing system may encounter limits that change as the system itself grows.

## Information, rules and goals
Information flows, rules, constraints and goals are structural variables. Decision-makers cannot respond accurately to information they do not receive, receive too late, or interpret incorrectly. Changing these structures can alter behavior without changing the physical elements.

## Resilience and leverage
Systems can adapt and reorganize. Leverage can lie in information flows, feedback structures, rules, goals and deeper assumptions. The hierarchy is a heuristic, not a guarantee; practical leverage must be assessed alongside feasibility, delays, resistance and side effects.

## Practical synthesis
Observe behavior over time → identify stocks and flows → map reinforcing and balancing feedback → locate delays, nonlinearities and limits → inspect information, rules and goals → test structural leverage and unintended consequences.
