# Critical Thinking — Mental Models

## CR-MM-01 — Ask “Is it true?” before “Can I defend it?”
Treat correctness as separate from advocacy.

## CR-MM-02 — State what would change your mind
A belief immune to evidence is functioning as an identity commitment.

## CR-MM-03 — Use the outsider test
Evaluate your own case as if it belonged to someone else.

## CR-MM-04 — Separate confidence from evidence
Confidence is a mental state; evidence is information about the claim.

## CR-MM-05 — Start with the base rate
Before explaining an individual case, ask what normally happens in comparable cases.

## CR-MM-06 — Inspect the anchor
Identify the first number, label or frame that may pull judgment.

## CR-MM-07 — Test salience against frequency
A vivid example is not automatically representative.

## CR-MM-08 — Reframe equivalent choices
If wording changes the decision, inspect the framing effect.

## CR-MM-09 — Check regression to the mean
After an extreme result, ask whether the next observation would likely move toward average anyway.

## CR-MM-10 — Slow down when intuition is unreliable
Use deliberate analysis when stakes are high, feedback is weak or the environment is unfamiliar.

## CR-MM-11 — Separate outcome from predictability
Knowing what happened does not prove that it was obvious beforehand.

## CR-MM-12 — Search for the strongest alternative explanation
A plausible story is insufficient if another explanation fits the evidence equally well or better.
