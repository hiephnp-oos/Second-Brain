# Critical Thinking — Concepts

Sources: Book 09 *The Scout Mindset* (summary source); Book 10 *Thinking, Fast and Slow*.

## Epistemic attitude
Scout mindset treats beliefs as models to improve rather than identities to defend. Motivated reasoning can distort evidence when a desired conclusion becomes tied to identity or status.

## Dual-process judgment
System 1 describes fast associative processing; System 2 describes slower effortful reasoning. This is a useful judgment model, not a literal complete description of brain architecture.

## Judgment distortions
Anchoring pulls estimates toward initial references. Availability can overweight memorable events. Representativeness can underweight base rates. Framing can change choices between equivalent descriptions. Loss aversion can magnify psychological weight of losses. Regression to the mean can mimic causal improvement.

## Confidence and narrative
Confidence and coherent explanations can exceed evidential support. Hindsight can make uncertain outcomes appear predictable.

## Practical synthesis
Separate identity from belief → define what would change the belief → inspect base rates and anchors → distinguish intuition from deliberate analysis → test alternatives → evaluate evidence before narrative coherence.
