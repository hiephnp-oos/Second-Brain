# Strategic Thinking — Concepts

Source: Book 15 *Good Strategy, Bad Strategy* — Richard P. Rumelt.

## Strategy kernel
A strategy contains diagnosis, guiding policy and coherent action.

## Diagnosis
Diagnosis simplifies the challenge around a critical obstacle. It is not a restatement of ambition.

## Guiding policy
The policy chooses the overall approach for overcoming the diagnosed obstacle. It is not merely a goal list.

## Coherent action
Actions should reinforce one another. Unrelated initiatives can consume resources without strategic effect.

## Proximate objectives
When the challenge is ambiguous, a feasible proximate objective can create a concrete path without pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

## Leverage and advantage
Leverage arises when focused action exploits a bottleneck, asymmetry, timing, coordination effect or other source of disproportionate impact. Advantage exploits meaningful asymmetry rather than generic superiority.

## Design, focus and inertia
Strategy coordinates choices to overcome a challenge. Focus requires exclusion. Organizational inertia explains persistence of obsolete routines and positions.

## Boundary
Game Theory owns interactive incentives and responses. Strategic Thinking owns diagnosis, leverage, advantage, focus and coherent action.
