# Decision Frameworks — Concepts

Source: Book 14 *Thinking in Bets* — Annie Duke.

## Decision quality vs outcome quality
A good decision can produce a bad outcome and a bad decision can produce a good outcome. Resulting judges reasoning from the realized result and creates hindsight distortion. Review the information and alternatives available at decision time rather than using the outcome as proof of decision quality.

## Probabilistic decisions
Treat decisions as bets: a choice commits resources under uncertainty and carries potential outcomes and opportunity costs. Important uncertain beliefs benefit from explicit probabilities or ranges. This makes disagreement inspectable, exposes confidence to challenge and creates forecasts that can later be calibrated. Expected value summarizes probability-weighted outcomes over repeated comparable cases; it does not predict the result of one case and is only as sound as its probabilities, payoffs and omitted costs.

## Belief formation and motivated reasoning
Beliefs are not formed from evidence alone. Repetition, availability, confirmation seeking, identity and incentives can make a claim feel true; high intelligence can help defend a preferred conclusion as well as test it. Ask what evidence would change the belief, consider competing explanations and use a concrete commitment or forecast to make confidence open to scrutiny. These practices reduce opportunities for error but do not eliminate bias.

## Decision records
Record the information, assumptions, alternatives, probabilities, confidence, reasons and plausible future branches available at decision time. This preserves the original information state and makes later review less vulnerable to hindsight and memory rewriting.

## Dissent and truth-seeking groups
An effective truth-seeking group explicitly rewards accuracy, welcomes diverse viewpoints, holds members accountable for reasons and evidence, and explores rather than simply confirms current beliefs. Membership or consensus alone does not ensure accuracy; groups can drift toward shared errors. Judge claims by evidence rather than the identity or status of the messenger, and make conflicts of interest visible.

## CUDOS norms for truth seeking
Use the scientific norms summarized as CUDOS: share relevant information (communalism), judge claims independently of the claimant (universalism), disclose and manage interests that may distort judgment (disinterestedness), and test claims through organized skepticism. Skepticism means making and engaging with arguments, not reflexive opposition or personal attack.

## Premortem, backcasting, scenarios and precommitment
Backcasting begins with a desired future and works backward to identify enabling conditions. A premortem assumes failure and works backward to expose plausible causes before commitment; using both positive and negative futures helps counter optimism and omission. Scenario reconnaissance maps multiple plausible futures and their relative likelihoods rather than treating a single forecast as certain. Precommitment, including Ulysses-style constraints and small friction devices, can protect decisions from predictable future impulses, but it can be bypassed and should be proportionate to the stakes.

## Emotion, tilt and time-separated selves
Regret, anger, fear and short-term rewards can narrow attention and drive reactive decisions (“tilt”). Add a deliberate pause, pre-agreed rules and a view from past and future selves to reconnect a momentary choice with longer-term goals. Such techniques improve odds rather than guarantee rational behavior.

## Feedback
Outcomes are feedback, not a complete score. Separate signal from noise and consider luck, skill, model assumptions, evidence quality and execution; most outcomes have multiple causes. Review the decision process using what was knowable at the time, take perspective on others' outcomes, and revise the process when warranted. The same result can follow from different decision paths. Hindsight erases alternative futures, so preserve forecasts and branches before outcomes arrive.
