# Decision Frameworks — Mental Models

## DEC-MM-01 — Judge from the information set available then
Review a decision against the evidence, alternatives and probabilities that were available when it was made. Do not import future knowledge into the original decision; outcomes are noisy because luck and many interacting causes affect them.

## DEC-MM-02 — Turn meaningful uncertainty into probabilities
State meaningful uncertainty with probabilities or ranges. Explicit confidence invites challenges, comparison of beliefs and later calibration. Treat probabilities as estimates that can be revised when relevant evidence changes.

## DEC-MM-03 — Keep a decision record
Before an important decision, record the choice, plausible alternatives, assumptions, evidence, confidence/probabilities, reasons, trade-offs and what would change the decision. Preserve plausible future branches to reduce hindsight and memory rewriting.

## DEC-MM-04 — State what would change the bet
Define evidence or conditions that should move the decision before seeing the result. Ask what you might be missing and which competing explanation could fit the facts. This helps surface motivated reasoning but does not remove bias.

## DEC-MM-05 — Invite informed dissent
Seek people who can expose missing evidence, alternative explanations and failure modes. Reward accuracy and exploration, not agreement or contrarianism for its own sake. Keep the discussion focused on claims and reasons rather than identity or status.

## DEC-MM-06 — Run a premortem
Assume the initiative failed and identify plausible causes before commitment. Treat the causes as hypotheses to test or mitigate, not as predictions that failure is certain. Combine this negative view with backcasting from a desired future.

## DEC-MM-07 — Backcast and map scenarios
Start from the desired future and identify the intermediate conditions/actions needed to reach it. Also map several plausible futures, including downside cases, and estimate their relative likelihoods rather than assuming one scenario is certain.

## DEC-MM-08 — Precommit against predictable drift
Set proportionate rules, friction or external accountability before predictable future pressure arrives. Ulysses-style constraints can help a future self follow long-term goals, but no commitment device is tamper-proof; consider how it could be bypassed.

## DEC-MM-09 — Score the forecast
Compare recorded probabilities with outcomes across repeated decisions to improve calibration. Use the result as evidence about the model and forecast process, not as a verdict from one observation. Expected value is a probability-weighted long-run average, not a guarantee for an individual case.

## DEC-MM-10 — Separate decision and execution error
A sound decision can fail through execution or bad luck; a poor decision can succeed through variance. Most outcomes have several causes. Attribute carefully instead of giving all credit to skill or all blame to chance.

## DEC-MM-11 — Update the process
After an outcome, examine what was knowable, how the belief was formed, which assumptions held, how execution went and what was noise. Change the rule or assumption that caused the error rather than merely switching the conclusion. Avoid self-serving attribution of successes to skill and failures to luck.

## DEC-MM-12 — Detect tilt and consult future selves
Notice when regret, anger, fear or immediate reward narrows attention and triggers reactive behavior. Pause, use pre-agreed rules, and ask what past or future versions of yourself would say about this choice. These methods reduce impulsive errors; they do not make anyone perfectly rational.

## DEC-MM-13 — Apply CUDOS in decision groups
Share material information, evaluate claims regardless of who made them, disclose/manage conflicts of interest, and use organized skepticism. A useful decision group seeks accuracy, viewpoint diversity, accountability for reasons and active exploration; group membership or consensus alone does not protect against confirmatory drift.
