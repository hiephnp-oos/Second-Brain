# Decision Frameworks — Lessons

## DEC-L-01
A bad outcome does not prove a bad decision; a good outcome does not prove a good decision. Evaluate the reasoning against information available at the time.

## DEC-L-02
Decision records protect against memory rewriting. Include alternatives, assumptions, probabilities, reasons and what evidence would change the choice.

## DEC-L-03
Probabilities make disagreement inspectable and learning possible. A stated confidence level is an estimate, not a claim of certainty.

## DEC-L-04
Premortems are most useful before commitment. Work backward from assumed failure to uncover risks, and pair this with backcasting from a desired future.

## DEC-L-05
Precommitment protects future decisions from predictable future incentives and emotional impulses, but commitments can be bypassed. Design proportionate friction and review escape routes.

## DEC-L-06
Postmortem review should improve process rather than justify the past. Preserve the initial information state and compare it with what was learned afterward.

## DEC-L-07
Separate variance from model, evidence and execution error. Outcomes usually reflect multiple causes; neither “skill” nor “luck” is a sufficient default explanation.

## DEC-L-08
Convert outcomes into calibrated feedback rather than emotional verdicts. Track probabilities across repeated forecasts; one result is weak evidence about calibration by itself.

## DEC-L-09
Expected value supports comparison by weighting outcomes by their estimated probabilities, but it depends on the estimates, payoffs and relevant opportunity costs. A higher expected value does not guarantee a better result in a single case.

## DEC-L-10
Motivated reasoning can defend beliefs that fit identity or incentives. Ask what evidence would change the view and actively examine alternative explanations; do not assume intelligence automatically protects against bias.

## DEC-L-11
A truth-seeking group needs norms for accuracy, diverse perspectives, accountability and exploration. Use CUDOS—shared information, universal evaluation of claims, managed conflicts of interest and organized skepticism—to guide its practices.

## DEC-L-12
Regret and “tilt” can compress attention to the immediate moment. Pause and consult long-term goals or future-self perspectives before acting under emotional pressure.
