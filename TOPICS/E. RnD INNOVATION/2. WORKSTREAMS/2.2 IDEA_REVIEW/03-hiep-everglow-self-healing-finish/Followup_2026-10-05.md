## 1. Executive conclusion

### Decision: KEEP — move to supplier/sample feasibility validation

The research changes the picture from “self-healing coating exists, but transfer to bathroom finish is highly uncertain” to:

> Commercial self-healing coating platforms exist, and there is direct technical precedent for clear protective coatings on Ni-Cr-plated and PVD faucet surfaces. The remaining question is whether a thin, optically neutral, bathroom-chemical-resistant self-healing topcoat can be engineered and qualified without degrading the underlying finish.

Strongest evidence:

1. NEI NANOMYTE MEND is a commercial self-healing polyurethane coating platform. NEI states that it is applicable to metal, wood and polymer substrates, can be applied by spraying, dipping or brushing, and can be customized.
   Source: https://neicorporation.com/products/coatings/self-healing-coatings/

2. NEI sells MEND in litre/gallon quantities and offers custom coating/sample services.
   Source: https://neicorporation.com/products/coatings/self-healing-coatings/

3. Asian Paints has a patent/application specifically covering a protective hybrid polysiloxane clearcoat for Ni-Cr-plated and PVD-coated faucets. It discloses spray/dip/bake application and a 5–25 µm DFT range.
   Source: https://www.quickcompany.in/patents/a-protective-hybrid-polysiloxane-clear-coat-formulation

4. EPA describes decorative chromium plating as a multi-step process: pretreatment, cleaning, acid dip, copper, nickel and chromium, with rinsing between operations.
   Source: https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-11/documents/b12s20.pdf

5. Hansgrohe describes bathroom PVD as an additional metallic layer deposited in a vacuum chamber, around 0.3 µm in its process description.
   Source: https://www.hansgrohe-usa.com/service/pvdprocess

6. Delta Lumicoat is positioned around liquid repellency, water spots, stains, mineral buildup and easy cleaning, not public self-healing/scratch recovery.
   Sources:
   - https://www.deltafaucet.com/design-innovation/innovations/lumicoat
   - https://www.deltafaucet.com/company/media-room/press-releases/primary-content-area/all-media-items/lumicoattm-finishes

## 2. Supplier / technology landscape

### 2.1 NEI Corporation — NANOMYTE MEND

Status: EVIDENCED — strongest first supplier candidate.

NEI states that its MEND platform is based on polyurethane, applicable to metal, wood and polymers, and applicable where clear/glossy appearance must be maintained. It can be sprayed, dipped or brushed.

Source:
https://neicorporation.com/products/coatings/self-healing-coatings/

Relevant variants:

| Product | Manufacturing cure | Self-healing trigger | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| MEND 1000 | Thermal cure | 60–80 °C | Hot-water activation is attractive; oven required |
| MEND 2000 | Thermal cure | ≥25 °C | Room-temperature healing; still thermal cure during manufacturing |
| MEND 3000 | Ambient cure | 60–80 °C | Avoids mandatory oven, but long cure time |

MEND 1000 published process:
- A:B = 1:1
- apply promptly after mixing
- transfer to oven within 5 min
- 80 °C × 15 min
- 120 °C × 1 h

Source:
https://neicorporation.com/products/coatings/self-healing-coatings/

MEND 3000 published process:
- A:B = 4:1
- dry-to-touch about 30 min at 25 °C for 1.5 mil
- ambient cure about 60 h for general use
- up to 60 days for full properties
- optional accelerated cure: 100 °C × 30 min after dry-to-touch

Source:
https://neicorporation.com/tds/NANOMYTE_MEND_3000_TDS.pdf

### 2.2 Autonomic Materials / No Corrosion

Status: EVIDENCED as technology/IP precedent; current commercial supply status needs direct confirmation.

The literature identifies AMPArmor self-healing coating families from Autonomic Materials. Current corporate/patent ownership has changed, so this is better treated as a technology lead than as the first procurement path.

Source:
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/marc.202200164

### 2.3 Asian Paints

Status: EVIDENCED as faucet clearcoat technology, NOT self-healing evidence.

Asian Paints has a patent/application specifically targeting a protective hybrid polysiloxane clearcoat for Ni-Cr-plated and PVD-coated faucet surfaces. The disclosed formulation is 2K/3K, can be applied by spray/bake/dip and has a disclosed DFT of 5–25 µm.

Source:
https://www.quickcompany.in/patents/a-protective-hybrid-polysiloxane-clear-coat-formulation

This is important because it proves that the architecture “existing faucet finish + transparent polymer topcoat” is technically contemplated for this industry. It does not prove self-healing.

### 2.4 Other technology leads

The self-healing polymer literature identifies NEI MEND, Autonomic Materials AMPArmor, SupraPolix supramolecular systems and Nippon Paint Automotive Systems CyGLAZ among commercial/self-healing coating technologies.

Source:
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/marc.202200164

These should be treated as technology leads, not automatically as qualified faucet suppliers.

## 3. Can it be applied to Ni-Cr, plated plastic and PVD?

### 3.1 Ni-Cr plated brass / zinc

Assessment: TECHNICALLY PLAUSIBLE — requires qualification.

The self-healing topcoat would sit above the existing finish:

Brass/Zamak → Cu → Ni → Cr → self-healing clearcoat

EPA describes the normal decorative chrome process as pretreatment, alkaline cleaning, acid dip, copper strike/copper, nickel and chromium, with rinsing between stages.

Source:
https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-11/documents/b12s20.pdf

The critical issue is adhesion to the exposed chromium surface.

Proposed process:

Ni-Cr plated part
→ final rinse
→ controlled drying
→ surface cleanliness verification
→ optional supplier-specific activation / primer
→ self-healing coating
→ flash / drying
→ cure
→ cosmetic inspection
→ adhesion / abrasion / chemical tests

Do not assume mechanical abrasion is acceptable because it may alter the optical finish.

### 3.2 Chrome-plated ABS/PC-ABS

Assessment: PLAUSIBLE, but thermal-expansion and cure risks are higher.

A conventional ABS chrome process includes etching/activation, electroless metal deposition and subsequent chromium plating.

Source:
https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=2000IITT.TXT

The proposed architecture is:

ABS/PC-ABS → conductive/electroless layer → Cu/Ni/Cr → self-healing clearcoat

The topcoat still sees a metallic surface, but the plastic underneath has higher thermal expansion and may impose more stress during hot-water and thermal cycling.

MEND 1000/2000 use an 80 °C + 120 °C cure schedule, which may be unsuitable for some plated plastic assemblies without qualification.

MEND 3000 is more interesting for plated plastic because it is ambient-cure, but its long cure time is a production concern.

### 3.3 PVD faucet

Assessment: TECHNICALLY PLAUSIBLE — highest adhesion/optical risk.

Architecture:

Brass/Zamak → Ni/Cr base → PVD decorative layer → self-healing clearcoat

Hansgrohe describes its PVD layer as approximately 0.3 µm in its process description.

Source:
https://www.hansgrohe-usa.com/service/pvdprocess

The Asian Paints faucet clearcoat patent directly targets PVD-coated faucet surfaces and describes adhesion, appearance and corrosion performance.

Source:
https://www.quickcompany.in/patents/a-protective-hybrid-polysiloxane-clear-coat-formulation

However, that does not prove that a self-healing polyurethane will adhere to every PVD chemistry/color/texture.

PVD should therefore be a separate validation matrix:
- polished PVD
- brushed PVD
- dark PVD
- gold/bronze PVD
- different PVD chemistries

### 3.4 Recommended test order

1. Ni-Cr on brass/Zamak
2. Ni-Cr on plated ABS/PC-ABS
3. PVD on Ni-Cr
4. Brushed/special-color PVD

## 4. Manufacturing process impact

Normal decorative Ni-Cr is already a multi-step wet process:

Polish → clean/degrease → acid activation → Cu → Ni → Cr → rinse/dry → inspection

EPA source:
https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-11/documents/b12s20.pdf

EverGlow adds:

Normal Ni-Cr
→ controlled final cleaning
→ optional activation/primer
→ mix self-healing coating
→ spray/dip
→ flash
→ cure
→ cosmetic inspection
→ coating QC

This is not simply “one additional chemical bath”. It introduces:
- coating mixing;
- viscosity control;
- spray/dip equipment;
- contamination control;
- flash-off/drying;
- oven or long ambient cure;
- thickness control;
- adhesion QC;
- cosmetic QC;
- VOC/EHS management depending on formulation.

Therefore process cost and throughput may dominate raw-material cost.

## 5. Thickness — major technical warning

NEI MEND variants with published DFT data specify approximately 1.5 mil minimum, equal to about 38 µm.

Source:
https://neicorporation.com/products/coatings/self-healing-coatings/

Hansgrohe reports approximately 0.3 µm for its PVD layer.

Source:
https://www.hansgrohe-usa.com/service/pvdprocess

Asian Paints' faucet clearcoat patent discloses 5–25 µm.

Source:
https://www.quickcompany.in/patents/a-protective-hybrid-polysiloxane-clear-coat-formulation

This is the main warning:

> Current off-the-shelf MEND should NOT be treated as production-ready EverGlow.

The target should probably be a custom thin-film formulation, ideally closer to the 5–15 µm range, while retaining optical transparency, gloss, adhesion, chemical resistance, abrasion resistance and healing efficiency.

The key supplier question is:

> Can the self-healing mechanism be retained at approximately 5–15 µm DFT?

## 6. Cost comparison — Ni-Cr vs Ni-Cr + self-healing topcoat

### 6.1 Cost-data limitation

NEI does not publish MEND pricing. It sells by quote, from litre/gallon quantities to bulk, and offers custom coating services.

Source:
https://neicorporation.com/products/coatings/self-healing-coatings/

Therefore all self-healing material prices below are WORKING ASSUMPTIONS, not supplier quotes.

### 6.2 Decorative chrome baseline

Public 2026 job-shop references put decorative chrome roughly around US$22–54/m² or about US$0.20–0.60/in², depending on process and order conditions.

Sources:
- https://www.clrn.org/how-much-does-it-cost-to-get-something-chrome-plated/
- https://quotebuddy.therness.com/en/blog/chrome-plating-cost-quoting

These are external calibration points only. Actual factory cost can differ substantially because tank utilization, labour, chemistry, wastewater treatment, yield and automation dominate.

### 6.3 Self-healing material consumption

At 38 µm DFT:
- dry coating volume ≈ 0.038 L/m²;
- at 33–36% solids, wet formulation requirement ≈ 0.105–0.115 L/m² before process loss;
- with spray/handling loss, a planning value around 0.13–0.16 L/m² is reasonable.

This is a calculation/assumption.

Because NEI does not publish a price, use a broad preliminary specialty-coating assumption of US$75–300/L only for scenario analysis.

At 0.13–0.16 L/m²:
- material-only ≈ US$10–48/m².

This range must be replaced by a supplier quotation.

### 6.4 Factory cost structure

| Cost element | Normal Ni-Cr | Ni-Cr + EverGlow | Increment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plating process | baseline | baseline | 0 |
| Final cleaning/activation | baseline | + | small |
| Self-healing material | — | + | medium/high |
| Application | — | + | medium |
| Flash/drying | — | + | small/medium |
| Cure energy | existing drying | + | medium |
| Labour/handling | baseline | + | medium |
| QC | baseline | + adhesion/thickness/appearance | medium |
| Waste/VOC/EHS | plating baseline | + coating-specific | medium |
| Capex | existing | booth/oven/handling may be needed | potentially high |

### 6.5 Indicative cost index

For early business-case screening only:

- Normal Ni-Cr = 100
- Ni-Cr + conventional clear protective topcoat ≈ 115–140
- Ni-Cr + self-healing topcoat ≈ 130–180+
- PVD + self-healing topcoat = likely higher qualification/process complexity

These are planning indices, not measured factory costs.

The likely cost drivers are:
1. extra coating operation;
2. controlled application environment;
3. cure/throughput;
4. cosmetic yield;
5. additional QC;
6. supplier customization/MOQ;
7. qualification testing.

The relevant business question is:

> Can EverGlow create enough premium value or reduce enough finish-related appearance/warranty problems to justify roughly 30–80% additional finishing cost?

That should be tested rather than assumed.

## 7. EverGlow vs Delta Lumicoat

### 7.1 Core difference

| Attribute | EverGlow | Delta Lumicoat |
|---|---|---|
| Primary problem | Minor scratches / micro-marring | Water spots / mineral buildup / hard-water stains |
| Main mechanism | Self-healing polymer/topcoat | Proprietary easy-clean / liquid-repellent finish |
| Scratch recovery | Target function | Not publicly claimed |
| Water repellency | Possible secondary property | Core value proposition |
| Mineral resistance | Possible secondary property | Core value proposition |
| Fingerprints | Not inherently solved | Delta says it does not actively prevent them |
| Appearance | Must remain unchanged | Delta explicitly says it does not change the underlying appearance |
| Bathroom commercial precedent | Not yet qualified | Commercially deployed |
| TRL | Lower | High |
| Differentiation potential | Higher | Already established |

Delta states that Lumicoat repels liquids, resists water spots, stains and hard-water/mineral buildup, and wipes clean with a soft cloth without cleaners.

Sources:
https://www.deltafaucet.com/design-innovation/innovations/lumicoat
https://www.deltafaucet.com/company/media-room/press-releases/primary-content-area/all-media-items/lumicoattm-finishes

Delta currently offers Lumicoat on bathroom faucets and hand showers.

Examples:
https://www.deltafaucet.com/bathroom/product/589-PR-DST.html
https://www.deltafaucet.com/bathroom/product/59808-PR.html

### 7.2 Where EverGlow could be stronger

EverGlow's differentiator is mechanical appearance recovery:

Scratch happens
→ coating partially recovers
→ scratch becomes less visible

Lumicoat mainly addresses contamination-related appearance degradation.

Therefore the technologies are complementary rather than direct substitutes.

### 7.3 Where Lumicoat is stronger today

Lumicoat already has commercial bathroom deployment, defined consumer benefit, multiple finish options and manufacturer-backed product positioning.

Therefore:

**Lumicoat = high TRL / established commercial proposition.**

**EverGlow = lower TRL / potentially higher differentiation.**

### 7.4 Potential stronger concept

A future version could target:

> Self-healing + easy-clean + low-water-spot topcoat

Potential benefit:
1. water/mineral resistance;
2. easy cleaning;
3. minor-scratch recovery;
4. premium appearance retention.

This is PROPOSED only. Surface-energy reduction, hardness, crosslink density and polymer mobility may conflict with one another, so the combined formulation must be experimentally validated.

## 10. Proposed first feasibility experiment

Do not start with a complete faucet. Use small coupons with controlled surface preparation.

| Coupon | Base | Finish | Topcoat |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Brass | Ni-Cr | None |
| B | Brass | Ni-Cr | Conventional clearcoat |
| C | Brass | Ni-Cr | EverGlow candidate |
| D | Brass | PVD | EverGlow candidate |
| E | ABS/PC-ABS | Ni-Cr | EverGlow candidate |

Test sequence:
1. Initial gloss/color/haze.
2. Cross-cut adhesion.
3. Pencil/nail hardness.
4. Controlled micro-scratch.
5. Scratch width/depth measurement.
6. Healing at 25 / 40 / 60 °C as applicable.
7. Hot-water cycling.
8. Cleaner exposure.
9. Wet/dry cycling.
10. Abrasion.
11. Re-measure gloss/color/adhesion.
12. Repeat scratch/healing cycles.

### Stop condition

Stop the path if:
- optical appearance is unacceptable;
- adhesion fails on the actual finish;
- healing requires impractical temperature;
- healing only works with an impractically thick film;
- chemical/abrasion resistance is materially worse than the existing finish;
- added cost is disproportionate to measurable user benefit.

## 12. Final recommendation

**Decision: CONTINUE — targeted supplier feasibility.**

### Recommended first contact

NEI Corporation.

Suggested technical request:

> A clear self-healing topcoat suitable for decorative Ni-Cr and PVD-coated faucet surfaces, with target DFT preferably ≤15 µm, transparent/high-gloss appearance, adhesion to metallic decorative finishes, resistance to bathroom chemicals/hot water, and measurable recovery of controlled micro-scratches.

> Invent a new self-healing coating.

> Transfer and optimize an existing self-healing polymer technology into a thin, optically neutral, bathroom-grade topcoat for faucet finishes.
