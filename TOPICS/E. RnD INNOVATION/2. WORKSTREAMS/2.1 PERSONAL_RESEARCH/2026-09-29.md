**Evidence status:** Commercial category evidenced; unmet user demand for a new architecture UNKNOWN.

- WHO: shower users who choose filtered-water or treatment-element products.
- CONTEXT: filtered shower products are marketed with replaceable filters and skin/hair treatment elements. A 2026 patent publication describes a showerhead assembly combining a water filter, treatment element and quick-release features.
- NEED / PAIN: replace or service treatment media without difficult disassembly or removing the shower assembly from the shower pipe.
- BEST EXISTING SOLUTION: replaceable in-line / showerhead filters and front-loaded or removable filter assemblies.

- REMAINING LIMITATION: evidence reviewed does not establish actual user friction, service time, leakage after service, replacement errors, or lifecycle cost for current products.

- Candidate architectures: keyed replaceable cartridge; captive service door with positive closure; service interface that prevents bypass/mis-installation. **PROPOSED**, not validated.
- User value: easier maintenance and more reliable treatment operation; demand and willingness-to-pay UNKNOWN.
- Fitting applicability: showerhead / filter base / waterway.
- Discovery disposition: **WATCH** — needs direct user/service evidence and comparison with existing products before idea admission.

**Strategic/Tier alignment:** Tier 1 — Showering Innovation & Technology / Wellness.
**Source application:** consumer showerhead assembly with water filtration and water-dissolvable skin/hair treatment elements.
**Physical principle:** mechanically separable module with alignment/retention features and a fluid path through a serviceable cavity.
**Mechanism / architecture:** WO2026096807A2 describes a filter housed in a base, front-loaded filter access, a treatment element in a handheld showerhead, magnetic docking, and quick-release features. This is evidence of a disclosed architecture, not proof of commercial performance.
**Demonstrated capability:** patent disclosure establishes a proposed configuration; independent serviceability, sealing, filtration performance and durability are not established by the reviewed source.
**Boundary conditions:** pressure cycling, seal compression set, scale/debris, incorrect reassembly, bypass leakage, potable-water contact, media compatibility, replacement interval, drop/impact, cleaning chemicals, manufacturing tolerance and cost.

**Candidate product capability:** serviceable treatment module with controlled reassembly — **PROPOSED**.
**Transfer risks:** architecture is already explicitly disclosed in a directly relevant patent; differentiation cannot be inferred from changing geometry or combining known features.
**Evidence:** WO2026096807A2 (published 2026-05-07); see Evidence Traceability.
**Main uncertainty:** whether measurable service reliability or lifecycle benefit remains beyond the disclosed architecture and existing products.
