## RUN_SLOT 01 — Market Pull emphasis

### MP-01 — Preserve acceptable shower performance while reducing water use
- **Strategic/Tier alignment:** Tier 1 — Showering Innovation & Technology; potential Circularity / water efficiency.
- **WHO / CONTEXT:** Household shower users using reduced-flow or water-efficient showerheads.
- **Need / opportunity:** Reduce water use without making rinsing, spray coverage, or perceived shower comfort unacceptable.
- **Existing solution:** Low-flow showerheads, spray-pattern changes, pressure-compensating and air-entraining approaches.
- **Evidence:** An older US government showerhead test-method report identifies spray adjustment, flow rate, temperature, hygiene performance and user acceptability as important but incompletely characterized parameters. EPA's draft WaterSense discussion also notes that lower-flow devices can be more sensitive to supply-pressure changes and discusses temperature-shock/scalding risks. These sources establish engineering/performance concerns, not current market demand.
- **Remaining DELTA:** A measurable performance envelope linking flow rate, pressure variation, rinse performance/coverage and user acceptance for a defined target use case. No new unmet demand is established by this search.
- **Required function:** Maintain a defined useful rinse/coverage outcome across a bounded pressure/flow range while reducing water consumption.
- **Candidate response spaces:** (1) passive hydraulic distribution that prioritizes useful spray zones under lower available flow; (2) pressure/flow-aware selection between spray networks. Both are **PROPOSED** and overlap known architectures in the current Tech Radar.
- **User value:** Potential water reduction without unacceptable loss of shower utility; willingness-to-pay and user acceptance are **UNKNOWN**.
- **Fitting applicability:** Showerhead spray plate / internal manifold.
- **Discovery disposition:** **WATCH** — opportunity is decision-relevant, but the baseline is crowded and a distinct, testable DELTA has not been established.

## RUN_SLOT 01 — Tech Push emphasis

### TP-01 — Flow-normalized pressure signature for estimating filter condition
- **Strategic/Tier alignment:** Showering Innovation & Technology; possible Circularity through maintenance timing, but applicability is strongest in recirculating/filter-based systems.
- **Source application:** Predictive maintenance for filters in recirculating shower systems.
- **Physical principle:** Normalize filter pressure drop against flow to reduce the effect of user-selected flow rate; track degradation and estimate remaining filter life.
- **Mechanism / architecture:** A 2025 thesis describes a flow-normalized pressure model and filter-life prediction evaluated using a dataset of more than 240,000 sessions, 700 shower devices and 850 filters. This supports a demonstrated data-analysis method in that system context, not validated performance in conventional shower fittings.
- **Demonstrated capability:** Research implementation for filter-status/remaining-life estimation in recirculating shower equipment.
- **Boundary conditions / transfer risks:** Requires suitable pressure and flow sensing, calibration across filter types and operating conditions, fouling characterization, reliable data, and a maintenance decision threshold. Transfer to a passive/non-electronic shower fitting is not established.
- **Fitting insertion point:** Filter module / recirculating shower system; not a direct showerhead-only insertion.
- **Candidate product capability:** Condition-based filter replacement indication that avoids premature replacement and detects flow degradation. **PROPOSED for target transfer**.
- **Existing-solution / Knowledge check:** TR-010 already classifies point-of-use shower filtration as BASELINE; this finding is a monitoring/maintenance enabler, not a new filtration idea.
- **Evidence:** Thesis link below; evidence supports the research method in its studied system only.
- **Main uncertainty:** Whether the added sensors, electronics and calibration provide enough user/lifecycle benefit in a target LIXIL shower architecture.
- **Discovery disposition:** **WATCH — TECHNICAL ENABLER**, not a standalone product idea.

### C-30-01 — Condition-based service indication for shower treatment filters
- **Discovery lane:** Tech Push; possible Market Pull linkage to MS-002, subject to target-system validation.
- **Candidate class:** TECHNICAL ENABLER.
- **Problem / opportunity:** Filter loading can increase pressure loss and degrade flow; fixed replacement intervals may not represent actual filter condition in every use pattern.
- **Existing solution / source technology:** Replaceable shower filters (TR-010 BASELINE); flow-normalized pressure and data-driven filter-life estimation studied in recirculating showers.
- **DELTA / transferable capability:** Potentially replace calendar-based service with condition-based indication. Benefit in the target product and user acceptance remain unverified.
- **Proposed response:** Use measured flow and differential pressure to estimate filter condition and signal service need. **PROPOSED**, not a validated design.
- **User/product outcome:** More appropriate replacement timing and fewer unexpected flow losses; requires comparative validation against fixed-interval replacement.
- **Mechanism:** Flow-normalized pressure-drop trend plus calibrated condition thresholds.
- **Fitting application:** Filter module / smart shower system.
- **Evidence status:** Research method in a recirculating-shower dataset **EVIDENCED**; transfer to target product **UNKNOWN**; product response **PROPOSED**.
- **Main uncertainty:** Sensor cost, accuracy across filter media/water conditions, calibration burden, reliability, and whether the user benefit exceeds added complexity.
- **Quality Gate:** **WATCH** — credible enabler, but target transfer, complexity justification and measurable user value are unresolved.
- **Discovery disposition:** **WATCH**; do not route to IDEA_REVIEW as a standalone idea.
- **Suggested next action:** Only continue if a target filter architecture and measurable service problem are selected; then define a bench test comparing fixed interval vs normalized pressure/flow indicator.
