## RS-10 — Market Pull Scout

### MP-01 — Shower diverter / selector can become difficult to actuate, especially with wet hands
**Status:** EVIDENCED signal; candidate architecture is PROPOSED.

- WHO: shower users operating multi-outlet or multi-function shower systems, including users with wet/soapy hands or reduced grip strength.
- CONTEXT: selector/diverter controls may require high force, have poor grip, or become stiff as internal seals/parts age or accumulate deposits.
- NEED / PAIN: switch shower outlet or spray function with low, predictable effort and positive feedback without needing a strong pinch grip.
- BEST EXISTING SOLUTION: rotary knobs, push/pull diverters, side selectors and mode dials.
- WHAT IT SOLVES: provides outlet/mode selection using conventional valve mechanisms.
- REMAINING LIMITATION: current user evidence includes selectors that are hard to turn or nearly impossible to operate with wet hands; some failures are associated with stiff or damaged internal mechanisms.
- MEANINGFUL DELTA: reduce required hand force and improve wet-hand engagement/position feedback while preserving reliable fluid sealing.
- REQUIRED FUNCTION: convert a low-grip user input into reliable valve/selector movement with controlled actuation force and a clear end-state.
- Evidence: current 2026 customer reviews and user reports describing hard-to-turn shower diverters/selectors; commercial shower selectors establish the existing baseline.
- External evidence: https://www.lowes.com/pd/KOHLER-Purist-2-In-1-Multifunction-2-5-Gpm-Shower-Combo-Kit/5013805941 ; https://www.lowes.com/pd/KOHLER-Maxton-Polished-Chrome-3-Spray-Dual-Shower-Head/1001277298 ; https://www.reddit.com/r/howto/comments/1wjnxby/cant_turn_the_shower_on/ ; https://www.reddit.com/r/mildlyinfuriating/comments/1rj5g8e/shower-knobs-that-activate-top-and-bottom-shower-heads/
- Existing Knowledge: TR-004 Bistable Compliant Valves is a WATCH / TRANSFER SIGNAL; TR-002 Pilot-Operated Diaphragm Valves is also a transfer signal for reducing actuation effort. Existing shower selector technology is already baseline/crowded.

## RS-11 — Tech Push Scout

### TP-01 — Bistable compliant / snap-through mechanism for low-effort state selection
**Status:** EVIDENCED technology; shower transfer is UNKNOWN/WATCH.

- Source application: soft robotics, compliant mechanisms, microfluidics and mechanical switching.
- Function: elastic deformation creates two stable states with a defined snap-through transition, reducing the need for continuous holding force and providing tactile state feedback.
- Physical principle: elastic energy storage, snap-through and bistability.
- Demonstrated capability: compliant bistable mechanisms are experimentally demonstrated for repeatable two-state switching; current Knowledge Sheet TR-004 records the technology as a transfer signal.
- Transfer boundary: shower applications require resistance to hot water, chemicals, scale, fatigue, creep, molding/process variation and long cycle life. The mechanism must also maintain predictable force after aging.
- Potential fitting insertion: diverter selector, spray-mode selector, push/rocker control or compact cartridge interface.
- Evidence: current Knowledge Sheet TR-004; recent compliant-mechanism research and demonstrated bistable fluidic valve products.
- External evidence: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06039 ; https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/13/3/1642 ; https://www.clarksol.com/shape-memory-alloy-bistable-valve-bv1101/

### TP-02 — Low-torque pressure-balanced rotary valve architecture
**Status:** EVIDENCED technology; shower transfer is UNKNOWN/WATCH.

- Source application: industrial hydraulic/manual selector and rotary control valves.
- Function: pressure-balanced sealing and low-friction/shear-type sealing reduce the manual torque needed to move a valve under pressure.
- Physical principle: pressure-force balancing, low-friction sealing and/or wiping seal geometry.
- Demonstrated capability: industrial water-compatible manual selector valves are commercially specified for low turning torque; pressure-balanced rotary valve designs explicitly use balanced sealing to reduce operating torque.
- Transfer boundary: industrial materials/pressures are not directly representative of shower cartridges; domestic flow, pressure, potable-water requirements, compactness, wear, leakage and manufacturing cost require validation.
- Potential fitting insertion: spray selector, diverter, compact rotary cartridge.
- Evidence: Parker Lo-Torq directional control valves; Fisher Vee-Ball pressure-balanced seal architecture.
- External evidence: https://www.parker.com/content/dam/Parker-com/Literature/Hydraulic-Valve-Division/hydraulicvalve/Catalog-sections-for-websphere/Republic/Catalog---Static-Files/8100E.pdf ; https://www.spartancontrols.com/products/valves-actuation/control-valves/ball-valves/fisher-vee-ball-v200u-rotary-control-valve/

### TP-03 — Vacuum-break / self-draining showerhead architecture
**Status:** EVIDENCED technology; fitting application is already crowded / DUPLICATE-WATCH.

- Source application: shower residual-water drainage.
- Function: admit air and/or open a drain path when flow stops so trapped residual water exits rapidly.
- Physical principle: gravity drainage plus vacuum breaking / air admission.
- Demonstrated capability: multiple patents disclose self-draining showerheads using air admission and drain paths; recent patents also disclose pressure-actuated air/drain opening.
- Transfer boundary: sealing under shower pressure, anti-splash behavior, hygienic protection and integration into existing shower geometries.
- Potential fitting insertion: showerhead body / waterway.
- Evidence: US20170341091A1, US20070194153A1, CN118635016B and related prior art.
- External evidence: https://patents.google.com/patent/US20170341091A1/en ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US20070194153 ; https://patents.google.com/patent/CN118635016B/en

## RS-12 — Idea Convergence

### C-01 — Wet-Hand Low-Effort Snap Selector
**Lane:** Market Pull + Tech Push / Recombination
**Disposition:** KEEP

- Problem / opportunity: shower selectors/diverters can be difficult to operate with wet or soapy hands and may require excessive force when internal resistance rises.
- Existing solution / source: rotary knobs, push/pull diverters and spray-mode selectors; existing baseline shower selector architectures.
- DELTA: provide a larger low-grip input surface coupled to a bistable/compliant force-transmission element that produces a low-effort snap between defined states, while the actual water-sealing element remains mechanically decoupled from the user's grip force where possible.
- Proposed product response: paddle/rocker or broad selector with compliant snap-through mechanism and a separate low-friction valve/cartridge interface.
- User benefit: easier operation with wet/soapy hands, lower required force, and clearer tactile confirmation of the selected outlet/mode.
- Mechanism: compliant bistability + force transmission / low-friction selector interface.
- Fitting application: shower diverter, handshower/rainshower selector, multi-function spray selector.
- Evidence status: EVIDENCED user signal + EVIDENCED enabling mechanism; combined architecture PROPOSED.
- Main uncertainty: whether the compliant element can maintain force/displacement characteristics over hot-water, chemical and high-cycle exposure and whether the resulting control feel is materially better than a conventional large lever.
- Suggested downstream prompt: IR-01 → IR-02; if mechanism gap remains, RS-03/RS-04/RS-06.

### C-02 — Pressure-Decoupled Low-Torque Spray Selector
**Lane:** Market Pull + Tech Push / Recombination
**Disposition:** WATCH

- Problem / opportunity: reduce operating effort of a selector while water pressure acts directly on the valve element.
- Existing solution / source: industrial low-torque selector valves and pressure-balanced rotary control valves.
- DELTA / transferable capability: use pressure-balanced or shear-seal geometry so line pressure contributes less to the user's required actuation torque.
- Proposed product response: compact shower selector cartridge with pressure-balanced sealing surfaces and a low-friction rotary interface.
- User benefit: predictable selector effort across operating pressure conditions and improved usability for wet hands or low-grip users.
- Mechanism: pressure-force balancing + low-friction rotary seal.
- Fitting application: spray selector / diverter cartridge.
- Evidence status: industrial mechanism EVIDENCED; shower transfer PROPOSED/UNKNOWN.
- Main uncertainty: package size, pressure loss, leakage after wear, potable-water material selection and whether the torque reduction is meaningful at domestic pressure.
- Suggested downstream prompt: IR-01 → RS-03/RS-04/RS-06.
